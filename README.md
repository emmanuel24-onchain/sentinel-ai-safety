# sentinel-ai-safety
An evidence led AI safety intelligence system exploring how to monitor emerging risks from advanced Ai through research analysis,model evaluations,verified incidents,and human review.
As Ai systems becomes more capable,understanding their risks require more than keeping up with new publications.it requires connecting evidence,examining what it means,and identifying developments that may deserve closer attention.

Sentinel Ai safety explores how this process could be made more systematic,traceable,and useful.
## THE PROBLEM

Important evidence about AI safety is distributed across research papers,model evaluations,technical reports,and documented incidents.These sources can describe different aspects of a risk,use different evaluation methods,or research conclusions with varying level of confidence.

Monitoring individual publications is not enough. A meaningful assessment may require comparing findings across sources,understanding limitations,identifying changes over time,and distinguishing genuine warning signals from incomplete or misleading evidence.

Sentinel is being developed to explore how an intelligence system could help address this challenge.
## The Proposed Approach
 Sentinel aims to develop a structured workflow for monitoring and analysing publicly available AI safety information.
  The proposed system would:
  * Monitor: identify relevant Ai safety research,model evaluations ,technical findings,and documented incidents.
  * Connect evidence: compare related findings across sources and track developments over time.
  * Assess potential risk: flag findings that may indicate emerging safety concerns or warrant further investigation.
  * Preserve context: Record supporting sources,relevant limitations,and uncertainty instead of presenting isolated claims as established facts.
    * Support human decisions: provide structured research briefs for human review before findings are communicated.

    The objective is not to predict every Ai failure or automatically determine whether a model is safe.it is to investigate wheteher better organized evidence can help people notice,assess,and respond to important developments.
    ## HUMAN REVIEW BY DESIGN
    Automation can improve the speed and consistency of research, but it can also misinterpret technical findings, overlook context, or generate misleading conclusions.

Human review is therefore a core requirement of Sentinel's proposed methodology.

Before a finding is shared, reviewers would assess its source, supporting evidence, accuracy, uncertainty, and significance. Automated flags would be treated as leads for investigation, not verified conclusions.

This approach is intended to make the system's outputs more accountable and reduce the risk of amplifying false alarms or overstating potential threats.
## RESEARH QUESTIONS
Sentinel will investigate several practical questions:

Can automated monitoring identify relevant safety findings across diverse public sources?

Can evidence from different reports be connected without losing important context?

How reliably can the system distinguish significant developments from low-value or misleading signals?

What types of errors, missed findings, and false alarms occur?

Does human review meaningfully improve the accuracy and usefulness of the resulting intelligence?

These questions will guide prototype development and evaluation.

Evaluation Plan

The initial prototype will be assessed against documented historical research findings and incidents.

Evaluation will examine source accuracy, relevance, evidence traceability, missed findings, false positives, and the quality of human-reviewed assessments. Where feasible, the project will seek external technical feedback to identify weaknesses in its methodology.

The aim is to establish measurable evidence of usefulness and limitations rather than assume the system is effective simply because it can produce automated reports.

## Current Status

Stage: Early research and prototype development

The project is currently defining its monitoring scope, evidence standards, risk-triage methodology, and human review process. A complete operational monitoring system is still on process.

## Development Roadmap

Define priority research sources and monitoring categories.

Establish evidence-quality and source-verification criteria.

Develop the initial collection and analysis workflow.

Design a structured risk-triage and reporting format.

Establish a documented human review procedure.

Test the prototype against historical cases.

Measure errors, missed findings, and false alarms.

Incorporate technical feedback and publish findings on the methodology's limitations.

## Intended Impact

Sentinel aims to explore a practical way of turning scattered AI safety information into traceable, reviewable intelligence.

If effective, this approach could help researchers and other relevant stakeholders identify important developments, understand the evidence behind potential concerns, and prioritise further investigation.

Its value will depend on demonstrated accuracy, transparent limitations, and whether its outputs genuinely support better-informed safety decisions.

## Principles

Evidence before conclusions.

Human review before findings are shared.

Transparency about uncertainty and limitations.

Evaluation against measurable outcomes.

Responsible communication of potential risks.

Sentinel AI Safety is an independent research and development project exploring evidence-based monitoring of emerging risks from advanced AI.
